The problem

Screen time limits are easy to ignore. Steps aren't.

Why ordinary app limits fail

  • You open Instagram or TikTok on autopilot and an hour disappears — sitting down.
  • Built-in timers show a warning… next to an "Ignore Limit" button. One tap and you're back in.
  • Hard blockers feel punitive, so you delete them after a bad day.
  • Meanwhile, the walk you meant to take keeps getting postponed.

The WalkScroll approach

  • Scrolling isn't banned — it's earned. Every step adds to your scroll minutes.
  • The lock is automatic and the exit is always the same: go for a walk.
  • Instead of fighting your phone habit, WalkScroll redirects it into a walking habit.
  • The result: less sedentary scrolling, more daily movement — without going cold turkey.

How it works

Four steps to smarter scrolling

  1. Choose your target apps

    Select the apps that eat your time — social media, video, games. These are the ones WalkScroll will gate.

  2. Walk to earn minutes

    Tap "Start Walking" and pocket your phone. Apple Health or Health Connect counts your steps and converts them into scroll minutes.

  3. Spend your earned time

    Open your apps as usual — a balance of earned minutes ticks down while you use them.

  4. Apps lock, you move again

    When the balance hits zero, your target apps lock automatically. The way back in is always the same: another walk.

FAQ

Questions before you download

Is WalkScroll free?

Yes — WalkScroll is free to download on both the App Store and Google Play. On iPhone, an optional WalkScroll Pro subscription costs $3.99/month or $24.99/year. On Android, the free version contains ads; a Premium upgrade unlocks unlimited target apps, a 120-minute time bank, and weekly history & challenges.

How does WalkScroll track my steps?

On iPhone, through Apple Health and your device's Motion & Fitness sensors — no extra apps needed. On Android, through Google Health Connect, which is battery-efficient. WalkScroll never tracks your location. Learn more about the step-to-screen-time setup.

How does the app blocking actually work?

On iPhone, WalkScroll uses Apple's native Screen Time API (Family Controls) — the same system iOS itself uses for restrictions. On Android, it uses Usage Access and overlay permissions to gate your selected apps. Learn more about blocking distracting apps.

Can WalkScroll see what I do in my apps, or share my health data?

No. On iOS, app selections are handled as opaque tokens by the system, so WalkScroll can't even see which specific apps you chose. Your step count, app selections, usage time, and preferences are stored locally on your device and are never uploaded or shared, per the privacy policy.

What happens when my earned time runs out?

Your target apps lock automatically. To get back in, walk to earn more minutes, spend saved minutes from your Time Bank, or — on Android — use an Emergency Credit or watch a short rewarded ad.

What is the Time Bank?

Steps you walk beyond your goal aren't wasted — you can bank them and exchange them later for unlock time when you need your apps without walking first. On Android, Premium extends the bank to 120 minutes.

Which devices are supported?

iPhone running iOS 16.0 or later, with Apple Watch support. On Android, any device that supports Google Health Connect. The iOS app is currently available in English.

My steps aren't updating — what should I check?

On Android, open the Health Connect app first to confirm your latest steps loaded there. On iPhone, steps track in real time. In both cases, check that WalkScroll has its permissions granted (Motion & Fitness on iOS; Health Connect on Android) in system settings. Still stuck? Visit the support page.
